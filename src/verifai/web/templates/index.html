{{ icon("i-chevron", "icon chevron") }}How it works
  1. Citations. Each DOI, arXiv id, PMID, ISBN, link, and reference is looked up in Crossref, OpenAlex, DataCite, PubMed, and Open Library. Retractions come from Retraction Watch through Crossref.
  2. Claims. Health claims go to PubMed, Europe PMC, Wikipedia, and fact-checkers. News goes to fact-checkers, GDELT, and Wikipedia current events. Everything else uses Wikipedia, Wikidata, fact-checkers, and pages you pasted. A claim in another language is translated so English sources can be searched too, and the translation is shown.
  3. Reading. An AI model reads each passage beside the claim and says whether it supports it, contradicts it, or is not about it. It only judges what was just retrieved; nothing comes from its memory.
  4. Two sources. A claim is supported or refuted only when two independent sources agree. The same paper in two indexes counts once, and one website counts once however many pages it returns.
  5. Keep going if one is down. A missing source lowers confidence. It never silently becomes the whole verdict.

{{ icon("i-check") }}Verified
A real record matches the citation.
{{ icon("i-split") }}Partial match
The work is real, but the title, year, authors, or journal printed beside it are wrong.
{{ icon("i-cross") }}Not found
No source has it. Often a made-up citation.
{{ icon("i-cross") }}Wrong paper
The DOI is real, but it belongs to a different paper than the one named.
{{ icon("i-ban") }}Invalid
The identifier cannot exist, for example an ISBN that fails its checksum.
{{ icon("i-alert") }}Retracted
The paper exists but was withdrawn. It cannot support a claim.
{{ icon("i-cloud") }}Couldn't check
A source timed out or refused. This is not evidence of a fake.
{{ icon("i-check") }}Supported {{ icon("i-cross") }}Refuted
Two independent sources agree the claim is true, or false.
{{ icon("i-question") }}Unverified
Fewer than two independent sources spoke to it. That is not the same as false.
